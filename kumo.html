Travel Journal
Japan Autumn 2026 · A story in places
Day 1
The city that never sleeps but always hums
Landed at Narita at 19:45. The humidity was different from Bali — still warm, but lighter. Took the Narita Express and watched Tokyo appear like a constellation rising from the ground. Checked into the Park Hyatt, 52nd floor. The view of Shinjuku at night is something I'll never forget. Everything is precise here. Even the taxi driver wore white gloves.
★★★★☆
Day 3
Fushimi Inari before the crowds
Alarm at 4:30 AM. No hesitation. Arrived at the base of the shrine as the sky turned from navy to tangerine. The torii gates — thousands of them, stacked impossibly — glowed orange-red in the early light. No tour groups. Just me, a few local joggers, and the sound of wind through cedar trees. I sat at a small fox shrine near the summit and ate a convenience store onigiri. Perfect.
★★★★★
Day 4
Nishiki Market and the art of eating while walking
The tamagoyaki on a stick is everything. Nishiki Market is 400m of organized food chaos — 400-year-old stalls squeezed into a covered arcade. I went in planning to browse. I left full, carrying pickles, miso candy, and a single chopstick I accidentally bought.
★★★★☆
Day 5
The deer do not respect personal space
The Nara deer are not the gentle creatures I imagined. They are aggressive, persistent, and absolutely wonderful. One ate my train ticket stub. Another headbutted a child for a cracker. Todai-ji Temple made me feel genuinely small for the first time in years.
★★★★★
Day 6
First onsen. Better than advertised.
I was nervous. Every part of me wanted to skip it. I didn't. The outdoor bath at Nishiyama Onsen overlooks a cedar gorge. Steam rises. Mountains. Silence. I stayed for 45 minutes and felt like a completely different person walking out. The best ¥1,500 I've ever spent.
★★★★★
